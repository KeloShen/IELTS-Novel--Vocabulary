**The Timekeepers of Absurdia**

**Chapter 1: The Punctual Panic**

In the **modern** city of Chronos, time was not just money; it was a religion. The **present** mayor, a man named Alistair Tick, was obsessed with schedules. He demanded **punctual** attendance at all meetings, to the **minute**. His **daily** routine was a legend, his **monthly** town hall addresses were spectacles, and the **annual** budget review was a gladiatorial event.

One **dawn**, as the first light broke, Alistair’s **permanent** assistant, Gloria, burst into his office. “Sir, the **up-to-date** reports are in. The **recent** audit shows we’ve spent a **billion** Chrono-Credits on sundials!” Alistair, a **former** accountant, paled. This was a disaster. “We need a solution **immediately**!” he cried. “**First**, call the committee. **Secondly**, prepare the emergency fund. **Finally**, we must **finish** this scandal before the **midday** news cycle.”

But there was a **prior** commitment. At the **opening** of the new clock tower, Alistair was to give a speech. Standing at the podium **initially**, he felt calm. However, in the **midst** of his sentence, he saw the **contemporary** art installation beside the tower—a statue of Elon Musk riding a **medieval** dragon made of melted watches. The shock made him forget his words. The **moment** stretched into an awkward silence. This **precedent** of public failure was his **foremost** fear.

**Chapter 2: The Intermediate Incident**

The **night** of the scandal, Alistair couldn’t sleep. He decided to work **overnight**. His **preceding** failure haunted him. **Incidentally**, while searching for the **quarterly** financial files, he stumbled upon an old scroll. It detailed an **ancient** pact with the “Timekeepers,” beings from a different **era** who had lent the city its first clock in exchange for **yearly** tribute—a **dozen** perfectly synchronized eggs.

He realized the **ongoing** budget drain wasn’t for sundials, but for this absurd egg tribute! The **duration** of this pact? One **millennium**. They were only three **centuries** in. The **imminent** threat, however, was the **anniversary** of the pact at **midnight** tonight. If the eggs were even a second late, the Timekeepers would reset the city to the Dark Ages.

“We need an **intermediate** solution!” Gloria yelled, looking at the **simultaneous** countdowns on seventeen screens. In the **meantime**, Alistair had to keep the city council distracted. **Meanwhile**, Gloria was tasked with procuring the eggs. The **instant** the clock struck 11:59 PM, she arrived, breathless, with a carton. But it held thirteen eggs. “A baker’s dozen,” she shrugged. “For good luck.”

**Chapter 3: The Everlasting Egg**

The **midnight** ceremony was held in the clock tower’s basement. The head Timekeeper, who looked suspiciously like Taylor Swift in a robe of swirling centuries, accepted the eggs with a nod. “Your **consecutive** payments for 300 years have been… acceptable,” she intoned, her voice echoing like a **million** ticking watches. “But the terms are **everlasting**. You must **periodically** reaffirm the pact.”

Alistair’s mind raced. An **everlasting** contract for eggs was absurd. He needed a **permanent** loophole. He remembered a clause in the **original** scroll: “The pact may be voided by a deed of **unprecedented** generosity.”

He acted on an **impulse**. “Oh great Timekeeper!” he announced. “We offer not just eggs, but the **foremost** culinary experience of our **contemporary** age!” He pulled out his phone, ordered a **dozen** gourmet avocado toasts for **overnight** delivery, and presented them **immediately** alongside the eggs.

The Timekeeper, a fan of **modern** brunch trends, was delighted. “This **generous** act breaks the **precedent**!” she declared. “Your debt is cleared. We shall return in another **millennium** to sample your **future** cuisine.”

As the Timekeepers vanished in a **simultaneous** flash of light, Alistair and Gloria stood in the silent basement. The **ongoing** crisis was over. The **duration** of the panic had been brief but intense. Looking at the **up-to-date** city clock on the wall, which now ticked normally, Alistair smiled. “**Finally**,” he said. “Let’s keep this **recent** event our little secret.” From that day, he was **periodically** seen smiling at **dawn**, no longer a slave to the **minute**, but enjoying the **moment**. He even started being fashionably late, just for the fun of it.
