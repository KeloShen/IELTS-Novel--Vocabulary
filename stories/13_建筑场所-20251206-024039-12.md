**The Concrete Dreams of Elon Musk, Mason of Mars**

**Chapter 1: The Foundational Fiasco**

Elon Musk, having grown **weary** of terrestrial ventures, decided his next great **enterprise** would be to **establish** a city on Mars. But first, he needed to practice. He chose a **site** in the Nevada desert, a vast, empty **expanse** perfect for a mock-up. His goal was to **construct** a **structure** that would serve as a **landmark**, a **venue** for interplanetary press conferences. The **design** was a bizarre mix: part **castle**, part **skyscraper**, with a **suspension** bridge connecting it to a fake **viaduct**.

He hired a **carpenter** named Bob and a **mason** named Brenda. “The **architecture** must defy Earthly logic!” Elon proclaimed, waving a **screw**driver like a scepter. The initial **stability** was a concern. Brenda insisted on a strong **foundation** using **concrete** and **steel** **beams**. Elon, however, wanted to **build** the first **storey** out of **brickwork** balanced on a single **pillar**. “It’s about the **aesthetic**!” he argued.

They began to **assemble** the **scaffold**. A giant **crane** lifted the first **column** into place. Suddenly, a loud *clang* echoed. The crane’s **apparatus** suffered a critical **malfunction**, its **hook** snapping. The **beam** swung wildly, threatening to **obstruct** the entire **operation**. “Do not panic!” Elon yelled, grabbing a **lever** from a nearby **shed**. “We must **fix** this **infrastructure** issue immediately!” Bob tried to **weld** the broken part, while Brenda **plumbed** a new support **pole**. The **erection** of the first wall was not going smoothly.

**Chapter 2: Dwelling on the Details**

With the **structure** finally **situated** upright, the team moved to the **interior** **layout**. The **living room** was **spacious**, featuring a giant **fireplace** made from polished **tile**. Next to it, a **radiator** from the 1920s stood, an **antique** Elon found **downtown**. “It adds **character**!” he said. The **inner** **corridors** were a maze. One **aisle** led to a **parlor** with a **cabinet** full of rocket blueprints, another to a **lavatory** with a golden **toilet**.

Elon decreed that the **complex** must have diverse **dwellings**. “We will **inhabit** this place to test **habitability**!” So, they built a luxurious **villa** wing with a **balcony** overlooking the fake **dam**, a modest **apartment** (or **flat**, as Brenda called it), a rustic **cabin** with a **chimney**, and even a **hostel** with bunk beds. Below it all was a **cellar** for **storage**, and a **nursery** where a high-tech **cradle** rocked automatically.

Bob, focusing on the **exterior**, worked on the **porch** and a **terrace**. “The **eaves** need to **trim**,” he muttered, sawing a **beam**. Elon wanted a **void**—a giant empty **room**—in the center of the building. “It represents the **void** of space!” The **ceiling** above this void was a grand **vault**, held up by a graceful stone **arch**. Access to the upper **layers** was via a spiral **staircase** with a polished **handrail** and a glass **elevator**. For some reason, Elon also installed an **escalator** that went nowhere, just up to a blank wall. “It’s a **metaphor**,” he explained to no one.

**Chapter 3: Urban Migration and Peripheral Panic**

News of the bizarre **project** spread. People began to **migrate** to the **vicinity**, hoping for work or spectacle. A small **urban** **settlement** sprang up on the **peripheral** land. A **mall** and a **supermarket** were quickly **built**, along with a **booth** selling “Martian” souvenirs and a **studio** for filming **promos**. A **casino** tent appeared, which Elon reluctantly allowed, calling it “**revenue** for **rockets**.”

The **external** **facade** of the main building was now complete, a shimmering **exterior** of glass and steel. The main **entrance** was a huge door under a stone **arch**. The **reception** **lobby** inside had a single **bench** and a fountain. To **enter**, you walked through an **airtight** seal, a test for Mars. The **exit** on the **outside** **wall** led to the **plaza**.

Elon decided to **reside** in the **mansion** portion. He invited Taylor Swift, whom he’d hired as “**Atmosphere** Consultant,” to **settle** into the **cottage** for a week. She took one look at the **shed** where the **carpenter** kept his **saw** and **drill** and said, “This is a **blank** space, baby, and I will write my **grievances**.”

Problems **persisted**. The **plumbing** in the **kitchen** and **bath** **malfunctioned**, flooding a **corridor**. The **basin** in the **lavatory** cracked. Elon ordered the team to **mend** and **modify** everything. “We must **maintain** **stability**! **Install** better **systems**!” He pointed at the **grid** of pipes exposed in the **ceiling**.

**Chapter 4: The Grand (Non-)Opening**

The day of the grand opening arrived. The **site** was now a **complex** of buildings—**shelters**, commercial **blocks**, all connected by a **grid** of roads. The **placement** of the flagpole was debated for hours. Finally, it was time to **furnish** the **interior**. **Cabinets** were filled, **radiators** bled, and **fireplaces** stocked.

The **venue** was packed. People **entered** through the main **entry**, **migrating** **inward** to the central **void**. Elon stood on a **balcony**, ready to give his speech. He leaned on the **handrail**. “Welcome!” he boomed. “This **landmark** **structure** proves we can **build** **habitats** anywhere! Observe the **span** of that **arch**! The **strength** of that **steel** **column**!”

He pressed a button to unveil the building’s name, lit in neon on the **facade**. Nothing happened. Then, a **spark**. A **screw** in the electrical **panel** came loose. The power died. The **elevator** stopped between **storeys**. The **escalator** jerked to a halt. The **interior** was plunged into darkness, save for the **glow** from the **casino** **booth** **outside**.

From the **outside**, **looking** **inward**, the **exterior** still looked impressive. But **inside**, it was chaos. Taylor Swift, trapped in the **glass** **elevator**, began singing a **melancholy** song about **failed** **launches**. Bob the **carpenter** sighed, pulling out his **toolbox**. “Time to **fix** this,” he said, as Brenda the **mason** nodded, tapping a **brick** with her **trowel**.

Elon Musk looked around the **darkened** **lobby**, at the **staircase** leading into shadows, at the **plaza** full of confused guests visible through the **windows**. He smiled. “Perfect,” he whispered. “The **malfunctions** are the best part. It’s so… **human**. Mars will be just like this.” And he walked **outward**, into the **desert** **night**, leaving his **concrete** dream **standing**—a little **crooked**, but **standing**—against the stars.
