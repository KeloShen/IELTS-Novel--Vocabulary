# The Timekeeper’s Terrible, Horrible, No Good, Very Bad Day

**Chapter 1: A Punctual Problem**

The **dawn** of a new **era** had arrived at the Chronos Corporation, though nobody had informed the head accountant, Barry. Barry was **punctual** to a fault; his **daily** routine was a **permanent** fixture, as unchanging as the **everlasting** stars. Every morning at the **moment** the sun peeked over the horizon, he would begin his **meticulous** review of time-based finances. Today, however, was the company’s 50th **anniversary**, and the **present** CEO, a **contemporary** tech mogul who had bought the firm **recently**, had decided to implement **up-to-date** and frankly absurd policies.

**Initially**, Barry was fine. He opened his **opening** report, comparing **monthly** expenditures. But then the memo arrived: all financial cycles were to be synchronized **immediately**. **Quarterly** reports, **annual** summaries, even **yearly** projections—all were due **simultaneously** by **midday**. Barry felt a cold sweat. The **duration** of this task would be impossible. In his **prior** role, such a demand would have set a terrible **precedent**. He longed for the simpler **medieval** methods of tallying sticks, a **former** life he’d only read about.

**Meanwhile**, in the **midst** of his panic, his assistant chirped, “The CEO wants to see the **foremost** profitable **century** of our operations. And he means the last literal one hundred **consecutive** years.” Barry stared. The company was only 50 years old. This was the **preceding** absurdity to what would surely be his termination.

**Chapter 2: Midnight Calculations & Overnight Fiascos**

By **midnight**, Barry was buried in paper. The **night** was **ongoing**, an **instant** of suffering stretched into eternity. He had to **finish** this. **Secondly**, after compiling the fake **century** report, he needed to draft a **modern** growth plan spanning the next **millennium**. His calculator smoked as he tried to convert **dozen**s of old ledger entries into digital format. A **billion** numbers swam before his eyes; no, wait, just a few **million**. It was hard to focus.

**Incidentally**, as he worked, he remembered the **intermediate** accounting course he’d failed. The **meantime** between that failure and now felt both **instant** and like a **permanent** scar. His **former** teacher’s words echoed: “Time waits for no one, Barry.”

Suddenly, a new email. The CEO, Elon Tusk (a man known for **simultaneous**ly running a car company, a space agency, and a boutique pickle farm), added a requirement: analyze the profit-per-**minute** during the **overnight** shift for the **preceding** five years. Barry’s **permanent** frown deepened. The **imminent** arrival of the **midday** deadline felt like a physical weight. He worked in **consecutive** hours, no breaks, his **duration** of employment clearly **everlasting** in its torment.

**Chapter 3: The Reckoning at Midday**

**Finally**, the clock struck **midday**. Barry, having not slept, presented his life’s work. Elon Tusk, a man of the **modern** age, scrolled through the digital report on a hologram. “Your **annual** growth projection for the year 3023 is based on lunar real estate?” Tusk asked, not unkindly.

“Y-yes,” Barry stammered. “A **contemporary** approach to **up-to-date** market trends.”

Tusk nodded **periodically**, every few **minute**s. “I see you’ve used **medieval** trade routes as a **precedent** for our **quarterly** logistics plan. Bold. **Initially**, I hated it. But **secondly**, I find its absurdity… **punctual**.”

Barry blinked. Was that a compliment?

“The **present** situation,” Tusk continued, “is that we are a time company stuck in the **past**. Your report, while factually a work of fiction spanning **century** upon **century**, captures a **modern** spirit. I need that spirit **daily**.”

Tusk stood. “Your **former** role is obsolete. Your **prior** task was a test. The **imminent** change is this: you are **foremost** in line to lead our new department of Chronological Creativity. We will meet **monthly**, brainstorm **quarterly**, and revolutionize **yearly**. Starting **immediately**.”

Barry, in the **midst** of this shocking turn, felt the **ongoing** headache **finally** **finish**. The **dawn** of this strange new **era** was confusing, but for the **first** time in a **permanent**-seeming **duration**, he wasn’t thinking about **midnight** oil or **overnight** shifts. He was thinking about the **moment**, and the **instant** possibility it held. **Incidentally**, he also made a mental note to never, ever use the word **billion** again unless absolutely necessary. A **dozen** problems were plenty.
